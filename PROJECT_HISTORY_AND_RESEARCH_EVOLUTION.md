# Project History and Research Evolution

**Investigation date:** 2026-09-16  
**Repository state inspected:** branch `master`, HEAD `2030a7c`  
**Purpose:** forensic history and evidence audit of the Batfish/Containerlab network-change research, including V1–V4, recorded analyses, public-data options, executed validation, and unresolved issues.

## 1. Executive conclusion

The project has produced useful negative and measurement findings, but it has not established a successful machine-learning result for residual network-change risk or transient disruption.

1. **V1 established plumbing, not a population claim.** Its small pilot demonstrated a working Batfish-to-FRR measurement chain, while exposing generator shortcuts and evidence/provenance weaknesses.
2. **V2 measured a nonzero false-pass rate, but the learned binary-risk arm was shortcut-only.** On the 299 decidable Batfish-PASS cases, 80 were harmful (26.76%). Generator class and fault family strongly recover the label; the learned arm was therefore not reportable as an operational risk model.
3. **V3 changed the target to a continuous, steady-state blast radius, but made that target analytically computable.** Batfish's saved RIBs reproduced the forwarding target exactly. The learned predictor did not add value over that deterministic baseline.
4. **V4 asked a different question: transient disruption during convergence.** The frozen 20-change pilot is a valid negative feasibility result: 99/100 population runs were valid, but just 1/20 change medians exceeded the preregistered floor, versus the 6/20 requirement. The frozen sequence stopped at D2/G1; D5/ML was not run.
5. **A genuine transient-loop case exists, but it does not rescue the population-level V4 claim.** In V4C-04, raw FIB replay reconstructs transient forwarding loops and subsequent recovery. The offline mechanistic audit finds the mechanism plausible but not sufficiently discriminative from predeployment data to justify a new ML campaign or runtime batch.
6. **Current direction: stop the present prediction program.** Preserve and publish the negative result and instrument. A new structural mixed-state convergence question is a possible *proposal*, not an approved continuation or demonstrated ML task.

## 4. V1 — apparatus proof and the first shortcut warning

### 4.1 Design and reported results

**RECORDED:** V1 is a single-topology exploratory pilot with 50 candidates, 41 distinct networks, and 9 fault families. The project history reports 38 HARMFUL and 12 SAFE outcomes. Its contribution was operational: establish the same-artifact Batfish/FRR chain, content digests, construct coverage, and a first end-to-end evaluation. It was too small and narrow for a general scientific claim.

The first clear methodological concern was that generated repair classes and feature patterns could expose labels. The Stage 1 report records shortcut AUC 0.999 on its Stage 0 slice; this is an early apparatus/corpus warning, not a result that should be attributed to V1's final population or treated as a V1 estimate. It motivated explicit shortcut gates in the larger study. This is a property of the synthetic repair-generation process, not evidence that such a classifier predicts real operational risk.

### 4.2 V1 evidence discrepancy

**VERIFIED-REPO:** The current V1 manifest lists 50 candidates. The current `phase2/batfish_summary.csv` contains only three rows (`C-0001` through `C-0003`), while `phase4/modelling_dataset.csv` contains 24 rows and `phase4/model_results.csv` 18. Project history reports 38 HARMFUL and 12 SAFE overall; model tables describe a 24-case PASS population with 12 SAFE and 12 HARMFUL. These denominators/status summaries do not reconcile from the current three-row Phase 2 summary, and no inspected artifact explained whether the Phase 4 data are a selected subset or whether the reports use different label definitions.

**Conclusion:** the 24-case model population is present in the Phase 4 data, but the current Phase 2 summary cannot independently reproduce the full 50-candidate verification or the reported PASS count. The discrepancy is disclosed, not silently repaired. The original Stage 1 report is about a later, separate V2 apparatus validation and must not be conflated with V1.

## 5. V2 — residual binary risk and the shortcut-only result

### 5.1 Corpus and measurement

**RECORDED:** V2 contains 1,479 candidates, 200 incidents, 20 topologies, and 10 fault families. Candidate repair classes are `correct` (420), `incomplete` (598), and `regressive` (461). Stage 3 was planned to reach 450 cases but stopped after 301; 149 planned candidates remain unmeasured.

The Stage 2 report describes 150 candidates: 109 SAFE, 38 HARMFUL, 2 EXECUTION_FAILED, and 1 NO_CONVERGENCE. A later PASS-conditioned interim analysis used 299 decidable candidates: 219 SAFE and 80 HARMFUL, giving:

- **P(harm | Batfish SPEC PASS):** 80/299 = **0.2676**.
- Wilson 95% interval: **[0.221, 0.321]**.
- Clustered-bootstrap interval recorded in the interim report: **[0.216, 0.323]**.
- The report describes this rate as stable across sample sizes (0.2635 at n=148; 0.2587 at n=259; 0.2676 at n=299).

These are corpus-specific measured proportions, not a production-network prevalence estimate.

### 5.2 Why the learned risk claim failed

**RECORDED:** On the correct 299-row PASS-conditioned population, worst-case one-vs-rest repair-class AUC was 0.961 (Tier A), 0.966 (A+), 0.977 (B), and 0.975 (C). The frozen shortcut-only criterion fired. `fault_family` was recoverable from Tier C at 0.9975. One generator class (`correct`) had 0/153 harmful cases, while `incomplete` had 41/73 and `regressive` 39/73; repair-class alone achieved AUC-ROC 0.8535 and AUC-PR 0.5550.

The cleanest reported feature set, Batfish-only `B_only`, had AUC-PR 0.291 against a 0.268 base rate (95% interval [0.226, 0.382], p=0.319): no convincing signal. A non-learned specification-widening rule achieved AUC-PR 0.8137, with 61 TP, 1 FP, 19 FN, and 218 TN. It was more useful than the shortcut-contaminated learned models for the corpus's actual failure mode.

Gate 0 randomized environmental link churn for 40 cases and found **0/40 label flips**. This supports deterministic labels in the tested setup, not an irreducible probability of harm. The proper surviving V2 conclusion is a measured false-pass/specification-completeness result; a calibrated residual-risk classifier is not supported.

### 5.3 V2 limitations carried forward

- The corpus is generated from a small set of repair operators; their configuration signatures leak repair class.
- Stage 3 was partial; no claim should imply all 450 planned cases were measured.
- Repeated or held-out splits do not eliminate generator leakage.
- The binary harm label is not an aleatoric outcome in the tested apparatus.
- Historic reports identify unresolved defects in analysis code (for example, suffixed banned columns and a hard-coded incident-count assumption). These were documented, not repaired as part of this investigation.

## 6. V3 — continuous steady-state blast radius, then analytic redundancy

### 6.1 Design and independent measurement

V3 changed the target rather than attempting to rescue the V2 label:

`r = (broken flows + rerouted flows) / golden-carried flows`

The design used 20 ECMP-free topology definitions, with 12 primary topologies and 8 held out. The 151-candidate built corpus used cost changes and blackhole routes, selected using an analytic severity model; all 151 passed the Batfish SPEC gate. The held-out eight topologies were designed for a future transfer arm and were not built into the measured corpus.

**RECORDED / independently audited:**

- Frozen verifier: 40/40 checks passed.
- 180 expected runs: 12 golden + 12 incidents + 151 candidates + 5 permitted golden replicates.
- 151 outcomes: 101 SAFE, 48 HARMFUL, 2 UNDETERMINED; no terminal run failures.
- Independent raw-RIB implementation matched the measured-r table for 151/151 candidates.
- 91 distinct r values; mean 0.116245; median 0.058333; maximum 0.447619; zero fraction 9.27%.
- Batfish route reconstruction matched 26,738/26,738 ordered flows on all 151 candidates.
- LOTO results recorded: Tier C pooled R² 0.848; Tier B pooled R² 0.744. The exact analytic/Batfish baseline is R² 1.0 for the target it computes.

The near-identity of the analytic model and measured target validates reproduction of this fixed FRR/RIB environment; it is not evidence of ML generalization. The predictor approximated a quantity already available from the verifier before deployment.

### 6.2 Gate and calibration qualifications

V3 amendments 12.1–12.4 are material to interpretation. Amendment 12.4 replaced a calibration protocol after initial results, with the original literal §8.3 recorded as failed. Under the amended analysis, Tier B received a GO under the specific calibration rule; Tier C failed calibration. Tier B/C model scores were also below the strongest non-learned lookup baseline in the project report. The resulting evidence does not justify expanding the corpus to learn this exact target.

Other important constraints: 12 synthetic topologies are from one hand-designed template family; only two mechanisms were built; mechanism and flow-type effects are strongly coupled; the corpus was selected on predicted severity; and raw Phase 3 runs are gitignored. These limit external validity and reproducibility unless raw artifacts are archived.

## 7. V4 — transient disruption pilot and frozen negative result

### 7.1 Question, target, population

V4 asked whether predeployment information can predict transient disruption while a network converges after a configuration change. Its primary target was:

`T1 = integral(broken_excess(t) dt)`

where `broken_excess` counts golden-carried flows broken at time t but not broken in the final state. This intentionally measures temporary disruption and excludes steady-state damage. The frozen run plan contained 20 population changes (four topologies × five roles), five replicates each; 10 no-op runs and four positive controls brought the planned total to 114 entries.

The frozen D1 floor is `0.05 / 72 = 0.0006944444444444445`; comparison is strict `>`. The population decision D2/G1 required at least 30% (6 of 20) change-level replicate medians above that floor.

### 7.2 Engineering amendments and launch

- **A3:** modeled silent kernel link-down semantics, added nexthop/link monitoring and replay checks, tightened validity/retry behavior, and anchored execution to freeze/provenance. Research question, target, population, and D1–D9 thresholds did not change.
- **A4:** retired two partitioning shutdown controls that had T1=0 by definition under final-state subtraction. Replaced them with the highest-betweenness non-partitioning links according to an analytic rule; retired raw evidence was retained. A4 was written after smoke 2 values were known. The predeclared hard stop required all four replacement/current controls to be valid and strictly above the floor in smoke 3; no further control replacement was permitted.
- **A5:** parallelized work outside the observation window after smoke-2 runtime exceeded the frozen D6 target. Poll intervals, observation window, target, replay, and thresholds were pinned and unchanged.
- Smoke 3 reported 153/153 validation checks and resolved the two open engineering blockers. The pre-pilot WSL preflight passed 32/32. Those are engineering/readiness results, not pilot evidence.

### 7.3 Pilot outcome against frozen gates

**VERIFIED-REPO and independently checked against raw summaries:**

| Quantity / gate | Observed | Frozen rule / interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Planned entries | 114 | 100 population + 10 no-op + 4 positive controls |
| Raw run directories / attempt directories | 114 / 118 | Retries and incomplete attempt are retained |
| Final accepted runs | 113 | One planned population replicate incomplete |
| Population valid | 99/100 (99%) | D8 requires at least 90%; **passes** |
| Changes with ≥4 valid replicates | 20/20 | D7 requires ≥4/5; **passes** |
| No-op controls | 10/10, all T1=0 | Supports the zero baseline |
| Positive controls | 4/4 valid and above floor | D9 **passes** |
| D1 floor | 0.0006944444444444445 | Frozen lower bound |
| Change medians above floor | 1/20 (5%) | D2 requires ≥6/20 (30%); **fails** |
| Within-change variance share | 0.0278317 | D3 limit 0.50; **passes** |
| Median runtime | 100.59 s | D6 limit 180 s; **passes** |
| D4 host invariance | Not tested | Single-host pilot; best possible outcome capped at GO-WITH-FIXES |
| D5 baseline/ML gate | Not run | Frozen sequence stopped after D2 failure |

Only V4C-04's replicate median cleared the floor. V4C-01 had one run-level value above the floor but its median did not; therefore six above-floor *runs* do not imply six positive *changes*. The population-level negative result is not invalidated by isolated positive replicates.

The four positive controls had T1 values 0.004738, 0.005067, 0.004927, and 0.005898 in the postmortem, all comfortably above the D1 floor. This, zero no-op values, and V4C-04's large valid transient argue against a generally blind instrument. The finding is that the frozen population/design usually produced less transient disruption than the frozen resolution threshold, not that no transient effects exist.

### 7.4 V4C-04 raw forensic example

The representative `V4C-04.rep1` raw replay contained 33 states from 32 eligible events. Selected flow histories:

- `corea -> wan2`: golden path `corea -> wan2`; at +0.410158 s the path was `corea -> wan1 -> corea` (LOOP); by +0.755588 s it was valid via `corea -> wan1 -> coreb -> wan2`; final state remained valid on a rerouted path.
- `wan1 -> wan2`: golden path `wan1 -> corea -> wan2`; at +0.410158 s it looped `wan1 -> corea -> wan1`; by +0.755588 s it was valid via `wan1 -> coreb -> wan2`.
- `coreb -> hosta` and `hostc -> hosta` also showed loops, including a second loop phase near +0.9832 s, and returned to their final valid paths by approximately +0.9835 s.

This reconstruction uses actual captured FIB events, not an analytic substitute. It supports the existence of an event-order-dependent transient mechanism in this case. It does not show that configuration-only features can predict event order, loop duration, or T1 across a population.

### 7.5 Evidence accounting discrepancies

- `V4C-12.rep4` has an `INCOMPLETE_AFTER_CHANGE` attempt with no `run.json`; it is marked unmeasurable in `validity.json`.
- The raw directory count is 114 and the attempt directory count 118, while `pilot_ledger.jsonl` has 117 rows and omits the incomplete V4C-12.rep4 attempt. The ledger should not be described as a complete attempt-level audit trail.
- Frozen launch approval, freeze, and preflight artifacts predate the pilot. They establish what was approved before execution, not the later run outcomes.
- The pilot results, ledger, postmortem, and mechanistic reports were untracked at inspected HEAD. Their contents were read without rewriting or adding them to the frozen commit.

### 7.6 Why this is a NO-GO, not a near miss

The preregistered D2/G1 gate failed exactly as specified. The subsequent postmortem attributes the low population response primarily to population construction: blackhole changes preserve final-state breakage and therefore yield zero T1 by definition; most cost/drain changes produced final reroutes without substantial transient breakage; and the population was selected by steady-state severity, not transient susceptibility. Positive controls used link shutdown, a materially different intervention.

The mechanistic viability audit reviewed V4C-04 and five exploratory comparison cases. A global directed-SPF model failed to reproduce the transient; a mixed old/new-state model could produce cycles but also produced them in the comparison cases; an asynchronous model did not uniquely distinguish V4C-04. Thus:

- transient-loop structure is plausible and directly observed in V4C-04;
- exact T1 depends on runtime event ordering/timing not available in the current predeployment feature set;
- the present structural susceptibility signal is not discriminative enough to justify ML or another runtime batch.

The pilot's correct conclusion is **NO-GO on the frozen population-level feasibility gate**, not an instrument failure and not an ML success.

## 9. Future research phases — proposals, not findings

The current evidence does **not** justify the original scale-up or an ML campaign. Any continuation below is **PROPOSED** and conditional on a genuinely changed research question.

### Phase 0 — archive and disclose the current record

1. Publish V2/V3/V4 negative and measurement results with frozen designs, raw-data hashes, analysis code, and failed/invalid attempts.
2. Resolve or explicitly preserve the V1 Phase 2 summary discrepancy and V4 ledger omission in an audit-trail addendum; do not overwrite frozen data.
3. Publish the exact pilot validity table and disclose that D5 was not run after D2 failed.

### Phase 1 — reformulate as structural mixed-state analysis

Define a deterministic staged-update model (router update order, old/new route state, forwarding-cycle definition, and what constitutes an affected flow). Test whether a purely symbolic graph algorithm can predict *structural possibility* without using T1, run order, topology ID, or hand-selected case labels.

The falsification condition is straightforward: if the rule does not discriminate across a broader, independently assembled case set—or a simple deterministic method captures the same signal—there is no ML contribution. V4's current mechanistic audit recommends STOP until this formulation exists.

### Phase 2 — external case-set and mechanism validation

Before any runtime experiment, define inclusion rules and independently source cases spanning topology families, protocol/construct types, and positive/negative structural examples. Cornetto could contribute repair diversity only where artifacts and license permit; LCORE-D/GNNet are not direct substitutes. Freeze features and labels before examining T1.

### Phase 3 — conditional runtime falsification pilot

Only if Phase 1–2 establish a stable, discriminative structural hypothesis, preregister a small, targeted runtime experiment with valid negative controls, repeated changes, auditable attempt ledgers, and—if claims include host transfer—the untested D4 second-host test. Preserve the existing V4 result and thresholds; a new question requires a separate protocol, not a retrospective revision.

### Phase 4 — conditional predictive modeling

Train a model only after the target is identifiable, independent cases exist, leakage controls are precommitted, and an analytic baseline is specified. Tabular models should be compared to deterministic rules first. A CNN is poorly matched to unordered network graphs. A GNN may be a declared exploratory ablation only if independent topology count and sample size justify it; it is not a primary recommendation here.

The pivot document discusses a possible scale of up to 48 topologies and roughly 1,500 changes. This is a **forecast/proposal**, not a power analysis, budget commitment, or endorsed next experiment. It must not be presented as approved given the V4 STOP conclusion and V3's exact analytic baseline.
